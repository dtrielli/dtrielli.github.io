Algorithms · News · Democracy

Daniel Trielli

Assistant Professor of Media and Democracy
Philip Merrill College of Journalism, University of Maryland

I research how news reaches the public in an increasingly algorithmically defined world, and how journalists can cover algorithms. My work examines how search engines, digital platforms, and AI shape journalism and political information.

Before academia, I spent about a decade as a journalist in my native Brazil, working at O Estado de S. Paulo and Diário do Grande ABC. I earned my Ph.D. at Northwestern University in 2022 and my master’s in journalism at Maryland in 2016.

Research

My interests include algorithmic accountability, AI and journalism, news visibility, and the relationships between media, technology, and democracy.

Visibility for Local News

Research on the visibility of local journalism on digital platforms, connecting questions about news distribution with the needs of local newsrooms and their audiences.

AI News Game

A tabletop simulation for teaching and researching AI in journalism. Players assign newsroom tasks to humans, AI, or both, and explore the consequences for reach, trust, and audience retention.

Supported by the Lucy Dalglish Faculty Fund for Media & Democracy, University of Maryland (2026).

AI and local journalism

Co-principal investigator on Employing Artificial Intelligence to Improve Local Journalism, supported by the Scripps Howard Foundation (2025–2027).
